## Script (paste into TTS as one paragraph)

In 1963, India's first rocket was carried to the launch pad... on a bicycle. Its office? A village church in Kerala. Sixty years later, that same country landed where nobody had ever landed on the Moon. So how did it happen? It started with one man, Vikram Sarabhai. He believed a poor country needed space, not for pride, but for weather forecasts, TV and farmers. In 1975, India's first satellite, Aryabhata, went up on a Soviet rocket. By 1980, India was launching its own. Then came the shocks. In 2008, Chandrayaan-1 helped confirm water on the Moon. In 2014, Mangalyaan reached Mars on the very first try, for about 74 million dollars. That's less than the Hollywood movie Gravity. In 2017, one rocket carried 104 satellites. Then, 2019. The Chandrayaan-2 lander crashed in the final minutes, and the ISRO chief broke down in tears. Four years later, Chandrayaan-3 touched down near the Moon's south pole. The first country ever to do it. And now? India has docked two satellites in orbit, sent an Indian to the International Space Station for the first time, and is getting Gaganyaan ready to fly Indians from Indian soil. Next up: its own space station by 2035, and an Indian on the Moon by 2040. From a bicycle... to the Moon. Follow for more.

## Story beats (for the reel build)
1. Hook: a rocket on a bicycle (1963) and a church as the office
2. Payoff tease: the same country lands where no one ever had on the Moon
3. Why: Vikram Sarabhai, space for weather, TV and farmers (not for pride)
4. Humble start: Aryabhata 1975 on a Soviet rocket, then own launch by 1980 (SLV-3)
5. Shocks: Chandrayaan-1 water (2008), Mangalyaan first try ~$74M < Gravity (2014), 104 satellites on one rocket (2017)
6. Fall: Chandrayaan-2 lander crash (2019), the chief in tears
7. Comeback: Chandrayaan-3 south pole landing (23 Aug 2023), the first ever
8. Now: SpaDeX docking (Jan 2025, 4th nation), Shubhanshu Shukla on the ISS (June 2025), Gaganyaan uncrewed test next, crew targeted ~2027
9. Future: Bharatiya Antariksh Station by 2035, Indian on the Moon by 2040
10. Close: from a bicycle to the Moon, then follow
