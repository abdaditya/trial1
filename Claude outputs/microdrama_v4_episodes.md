# Microdramas, told as a microdrama: "The 50-cent cliffhanger" (v4)

The angle is **one deep dive**: how a one-minute show is engineered to make you pay. We follow one viewer, Riya, through one night. It opens with "The microdrama business, explained. In six episodes." Then the reel plays like a microdrama app: 6 episodes, each with a title card, each ending on a freeze-frame cliffhanger and a swipe-up into the next.

Length: ~285 words, about 1:40–1:45. Episode titles are spoken ("Episode one. The Ad.") and shown on screen as the title card.

---

## Voiceover

The microdrama business, explained. In six episodes.

**Episode one. The Ad.**
Eleven fifty-two at night. Riya is scrolling Instagram. An ad plays. A bride is thrown out in the rain. A black car stops. The window rolls down... and the ad ends.
She taps. She just walked into a machine built for exactly that second.

**Episode two. Free.**
The first ten episodes are free. Each one, ninety seconds. And every single one ends mid-slap, mid-kiss, mid-secret.
Nothing here is written to be good. It's written to make you swipe.

**Episode three. The Paywall.**
Episode eleven. The stranger is about to reveal who he really is. The screen freezes.
Sixty coins. About fifty cents.
She pays. By two a.m., she's unlocked forty more. Around twenty dollars for one show.

**Episode four. Behind the Camera.**
What she paid for: one rented mansion, shot in about a week, for roughly two hundred thousand dollars.
And more than half of Riya's money goes straight into ads, to catch the next Riya.
ReelShort, the biggest app, went from ninety-seven million dollars to seven hundred and eighty-five million in two years.

**Episode five. Millions of Riyas.**
In China, these one-minute shows already earn more than every cinema in the country.
In India, a hundred million people watch every month. Kuku's revenue jumped almost six times, to fourteen hundred crore, and it's heading for an IPO.

**Episode six. The Twist.**
But Indians pay less. So apps are cutting out the most expensive part. The actors. The sets. The crew.
A show that cost twenty lakh rupees now costs two, made with AI.
So the next billionaire Riya falls for... might not exist at all.
To be continued. Would you pay to see what happens next?

Follow for the real stories behind the biggest deals.

## Animation plan (episode by episode)

**Cold open (0–4 s):** "The microdrama business, explained." A big title, with the app's 6-segment progress bar drawing in under it and "6 EPISODES" ticking up.

**The frame running through the whole reel:**
- The reel looks like a (fictional) microdrama app.
- Top progress bar: 6 segments that fill up as each episode plays.
- Each episode opens with a title card: a slammed-in "EP 3 · THE PAYWALL" with a film-grain flash.
- Each episode ends with a freeze-frame, a red "NEXT EPISODE" chip, and a **swipe-up transition**: a vertical whip with motion blur, the real app gesture.

**EP 1, The Ad:**
- A dark bedroom with a 3D phone floating in blue light (three.js) and a clock reading 11:52.
- The camera dives *into* the screen in one continuous zoom, and the drama fills the frame. It is built by us, not a real show clip:
  - rain particles and a bride silhouette on the steps;
  - headlights sweeping across;
  - a car window rolling down.
- Hard cut to black: "AD ENDED". A tap ripple.

**EP 2, Free:**
- A 3D carousel of 10 vertical episode thumbnails flies past.
- Under it, a heartbeat-style "tension line" spikes at the end of every episode. Each spike carries one word: SLAP, KISS, SECRET.
- The line is rendered as a real ECG monitor.

**EP 3, The Paywall:**
- The reveal freezes and the frame desaturates and cracks.
- A paywall sheet slides up: "Unlock EP 11 · 60 coins". A thumb presses, coins spill out.
- The clock spins from 11:52 to 2:00 AM while a $0 → $20 counter runs and the coins stack up in 3D.

**EP 4, Behind the Camera:**
- The camera pulls back *out* of the phone and reveals the set as a cutaway diorama (like the magnet factory, which you liked): one mansion room, lights, one camera.
- A 7-day calendar flips and a clapboard slams with a counter running to 60 episodes, ending on a $200K tag.
- Riya's $20 coin stack splits: 55% flows into the Facebook, TikTok, Snapchat and Instagram logos, becomes a new ad, and lands on *another* phone. The money loop is shown literally.
- The ReelShort bars rise: $97M → $785M.

**EP 5, Millions of Riyas:**
- The kickass moment: one phone multiplies into thousands of glowing phones (three.js instanced) that fly into the **outline of India**, with the counter running to 100M.
- A whip across to China: the phones form a cinema screen, and the ¥50.4B vs ¥42.5B bars show microdramas beating the box office.
- The Kuku bar ₹240 Cr → ₹1,400 Cr, then an IPO bell.

**EP 6, The Twist:**
- Back inside the drama, the actors' faces turn to wireframe mesh and then pixels (the AI dissolve).
- The ₹20 lakh price tag shatters into ₹2 lakh.
- The billionaire glitches out of the frame on "might not exist at all".
- A final freeze, then "TO BE CONTINUED…" in drama-title style.
- The end card's FOLLOW button is styled as "Unlock next episode".

## Fact notes

- **ReelShort pricing:**
  - About the first 8–13 episodes are free.
  - A standard episode costs 60 coins; coins cost about $0.007 each in the biggest pack (so about $0.42 per episode), more in smaller packs.
  - About 50 episodes cost close to $30. (ReelShort's own fan site.)
- **Production:**
  - About $150k–250k per series, shot in about a week, 50–70 episodes (earlier research).
  - India: 60–90 s episodes, often 60–100 per series (FICCI Frames 2026).
- **Ads and revenue:**
  - Marketing took about 55% of ReelShort's 2025 revenue.
  - Revenue went $97M (2023) → $785M (2025).
- **China:** about ¥50.4B microdrama revenue vs a ¥42.5B box office (2024).
- **India:**
  - About 100M monthly users (Business Today, Jun 2026).
  - Kuku's revenue went ₹240 Cr → ₹1,400 Cr (FY25 → FY26), and it has filed a confidential DRHP.
  - A show costs ₹20–25 lakh normally, or ₹2–3 lakh with AI (The Hollywood Reporter India).
- **Fiction:** "Riya" and the show she watches are fictional. The drama scenes are our own silhouette and 3D builds, not real show footage.
