# If AI can build software, why buy it? (The SaaSpocalypse) — Voiceover v2

**Length:** about 285 words, roughly 95–100 seconds (Google × Wiz pace).

---

In February 2026, two hundred and eighty-five billion dollars of software stocks vanished in just two days.
Not because of a recession. Because of an AI plugin.

And it all comes down to one question: if AI can build software for you… why would you ever pay for it again?

It started in 2024. The CEO of Klarna said his company had switched off Salesforce, and replaced it with AI they built themselves.
Wall Street heard that, and started to panic.

Because companies like Salesforce, Adobe and Atlassian make money in one simple way.
They rent you software, and charge for every employee who uses it. Every seat is one more bill.

And AI was coming for both sides.
It could build the software. And it could do the employee's job, so you'd need fewer seats.
So through 2025, investors quietly started selling. Adobe, Salesforce and Atlassian each lost around a third of their value.

Then in February, the fear became real.
Anthropic released AI agents that could do legal, sales and finance work on their own.
That's when those two hundred and eighty-five billion dollars disappeared. Thomson Reuters, the company lawyers pay for research, crashed sixteen percent.
Wall Street gave it a name: the SaaSpocalypse.

But the panic missed one thing.
Klarna's CEO later admitted they never really replaced Salesforce with AI. They moved their data into a system they built themselves.
Because the hard part of software was never the code. It's the data. Years of customers, deals and invoices, and the trust to keep them safe.

And AI needs that data to do anything useful.
So instead of killing Salesforce, Anthropic partnered with it. Claudeforce put Claude right inside Salesforce and Slack.
Then the earnings came in. Atlassian's stock nearly tripled from its low, and software stocks are up more than forty-five percent since April.

But not everyone is forgiven. Adobe grew its AI revenue a hundred and fifty percent, and its stock still fell.

So is the SaaSpocalypse over… or has it just started? Tell me in the comments.

Follow for the real stories behind the biggest deals.

**Fact notes:**

- **Klarna:**
  - In Sept 2024 CEO Sebastian Siemiatkowski said Klarna had shut down Salesforce (and planned to drop Workday), replacing them with in-house AI-driven tech.
  - In Mar 2025 he clarified: "we did not replace SaaS with an LLM." Klarna had consolidated data from several SaaS tools into its own stack (Neo4j and others).
- **2025, full year:** Adobe −35%, Atlassian −34%, Salesforce −31% (HubSpot −51%, Monday.com −36%), because of the risk to seat-based pricing.
- **February 2026:**
  - Claude Cowork launched on 12 Jan, and 11 plugins followed on 30 Jan (legal, sales, finance and more).
  - About $285B of software market value was lost in roughly 48 hours around 3–4 Feb.
  - Thomson Reuters fell about 16%, LegalZoom nearly 20%, and RELX and FactSet had double-digit drops.
- **The bottom:** the IGV software ETF fell 36.6% from its 22 Nov 2025 peak to its 10 Apr 2026 low.
- **The comeback:**
  - IGV is up about 46% from the April low, about 7.5% below its record.
  - Atlassian fell 66.5%, then rallied about 194%; its Aug 6 quarter showed revenue +28%.
  - Salesforce Q2 FY27 (26 Aug): Agentforce ARR $1.5B (+240%), and the stock rose about 10%.
- **Claudeforce (announced 26–28 Aug 2026):**
  - Claude powers Agentforce's reasoning and is the default model in Slack, and there's a Salesforce plugin inside Claude.
  - Benioff: "This nonsense of the SaaSpocalypse, I think it's time for it to stop."
- **Adobe Q3 FY26 (Sept):** revenue $6.76B (+13%), AI-first ARR above $650M (+150%). The stock still fell about 2%, and a CEO transition was announced.
