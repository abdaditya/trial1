# The microdrama business, explained (v5)

**Format:** the reel looks like a microdrama app. There are 6 "episodes", each covering one part of the business. The episode titles appear **only on screen**, as a title card plus a 6-segment progress bar. The voiceover never says "episode". Each part ends on a small cliffhanger line and an app-style swipe-up into the next.

**Length:** about 304 words, roughly 1:40.

---

## Voiceover

A one-minute soap opera, shot in a week, now makes more money in China than every movie in every cinema.
The microdrama business, explained.

*[on screen: EP 1 · THE FORMULA]*
Every episode is about ninety seconds, and one series runs sixty to a hundred of them.
Each one ends on a cliffhanger. A slap. A secret. A billionaire in disguise.

*[EP 2 · THE PAYWALL]*
The first ten or so are free. Then, right before the big reveal, the story freezes.
Sixty coins to unlock. About fifty cents.
Finish one show, and you've paid twenty to thirty dollars.

*[EP 3 · THE FACTORY]*
Making it? Around two hundred thousand dollars, shot in about a week.
That's roughly two thousand dollars per minute of screen time.

*[EP 4 · THE AD MACHINE]*
So where does the money go? Into ads.
ReelShort, the biggest app, spent fifty-five cents of every dollar on marketing.
And the ad is just the free episodes, cut right before the cliffhanger.
It even pushes you to pay on its website, to skip Apple and Google's thirty percent cut.

*[EP 5 · THE CATCH]*
It works. Revenue jumped from ninety-seven million dollars to seven hundred and eighty-five million in two years.
Yet it was still estimated to be losing money.
Because this is really an ad bet: every dollar spent on ads has to bring back more than a dollar.

*[EP 6 · INDIA]*
Now the fight is moving to India.
A hundred million Indians watch every month. But only seventeen million pay, and they pay less than half of what streaming earns.
So Indian apps win on cost, not price.
Kuku releases over a hundred and fifty shows a month, grew revenue almost six times to fourteen hundred crore, and is heading for an IPO.
And with AI, a twenty-lakh-rupee show now costs two.

The next hit might not need a single actor.
Would you pay fifty cents to see what happens next?

Follow for the real stories behind the biggest deals.

## Fact notes

- **China:** microdrama revenue about ¥50.4B vs a ¥42.5B box office (2024).
- **Format:** 60–90 s episodes, often 60–100 per series (FICCI Frames 2026).
- **ReelShort pricing** (ReelShort's own fan site):
  - About the first 8–13 episodes are free.
  - 60 coins per episode, about $0.42–0.60 depending on the coin pack.
  - About 50 episodes cost close to $30.
  - A weekly subscription is $20+ (Consume Our Internet).
- **Production:** about $150k–250k per series, shot in about a week. At 60–100 episodes of 60–90 s, that is roughly 60–150 minutes, so about $2k per minute.
- **ReelShort:**
  - Marketing was about 55% of 2025 revenue.
  - Revenue went $97M (2023) → $785M (2025), with an estimated ~$12M loss in 2025.
  - It pushes web payments to avoid the app stores' commission of up to 30%.
- **India:**
  - 100M monthly active users and 17M paying users.
  - ARPU is about $15, against about $35 for OTT streaming (Business Today, Jun 2026).
- **Kuku:**
  - Revenue went ₹240 Cr → ₹1,400 Cr (FY25 → FY26), and it has filed a confidential DRHP.
  - It makes 150+ original shows a month (FICCI Frames 2026).
- **AI:** a show costs ₹20–25 lakh normally, or ₹2–3 lakh with AI (The Hollywood Reporter India).
