# Microdramas: the one-minute shows beating Hollywood — Voiceover v3

**Length:** 271 words, roughly 90 seconds.

**"Close to nine billion" =** China ~¥50.4B ≈ $7B (2024) + US ~$1.3B/yr (2025) + India ~$0.3B (2025) ≈ $8.6B.

---

One-minute soap operas, on your phone.
They're called microdramas. And across China, America and India, they now make close to nine billion dollars a year.
China alone spends more on them than on its entire box office.

So how does a one-minute soap opera make that much money?

A billionaire in disguise. A betrayed wife. Every episode ends on a cliffhanger.
The first few are free. Then, right before the big slap, a paywall. Thirty cents to see what happens next.
By the finale, a fan has paid up to thirty dollars for one show, shot in a week for about two hundred thousand.

Hollywood tried this first. Quibi raised one point seven five billion dollars, made expensive shows, and died in six months.
The microdrama apps did the opposite. Cheap shows. Huge ad budgets.
ReelShort spends fifty-five cents of every dollar on Facebook and TikTok ads, selling you the slap, not the ending.
Its revenue jumped from ninety-seven million dollars in 2023 to seven hundred and eighty-five million last year.

And the fastest-growing market now is India.
A hundred million Indians watch every month.
Kuku TV's parent grew revenue almost six times in one year, to fourteen hundred crore rupees, and has filed for an IPO.

But a paying Indian viewer brings in just fifteen dollars, less than half of streaming.
So in India, the winner won't make the best stories. It'll make the cheapest. And that race will be won by AI.

The next blockbuster might be one minute long.
Would you pay thirty cents to see what happens next?

Follow for the real stories behind the biggest deals.

**Fact notes:**

- **Quibi:**
  - Raised $1.75B and launched in April 2020.
  - It announced its shutdown in October 2020, about 6 months after launch.
- **ReelShort:**
  - Launched in Aug 2022 by Crazy Maple Studio (backed by Beijing's COL Group).
  - Revenue: $97M (2023) → $400M (2024) → $785M (2025, with an estimated ~$12M loss).
  - Media Partners Asia projects $1.05B revenue and about $40M net profit for 2026 (Variety, Aug 2026).
  - Marketing took about 55% of 2025 revenue. By channel: Facebook 25%, TikTok 19%, Snapchat 16%, Instagram 8%.
  - It now sells through web payments to avoid the app stores' up-to-30% commission.
  - Viewers watch 35.7 min a day, against Netflix's 24.8 min on mobile.
  - It holds about 29% global share, ahead of DramaBox (21%).
  - Hit shows include "The Double Life of My Billionaire Husband" (500M+ views).
- **China:**
  - 2024 microdrama revenue was about ¥50.4B, against the 2024 box office of ¥42.5B.
  - About 662M viewers.
- **Pricing and costs:**
  - Episodes cost about $0.30–0.50, and finishing one show costs about $20–30 (2023 data).
  - Shows are made for about $150k–250k over roughly a week for 50–70 episodes.
  - AI has cut production costs by up to about 90% (ReelShort / MPA).
- **Hollywood:**
  - Fox Entertainment took a stake in Holywater (the My Drama app) to make 200+ vertical series.
  - Disney picked DramaBox for its Accelerator.
- **India overall (Business Today, Jun 2026):**
  - More than $300M revenue in 2025.
  - 100M monthly active users and 17M paying users.
  - 450M cumulative downloads and about 60 min a day of viewing.
  - ARPU is about $15 per paying user, against about $35 for OTT streaming.
  - India made up 22% of global short-drama app downloads in Q1 2026 (Sensor Tower).
- **JioHotstar Tadka:** reported 100M users in 2026 (Variety / The Hollywood Reporter).
- **Kuku:**
  - Revenue went from ₹240 Cr in FY25 to ₹1,400 Cr in FY26 (5.8×), close to breakeven.
  - It has filed a confidential DRHP for a ₹3,500 Cr IPO at a target of about ₹15,000 Cr.
  - Kuku TV has 100M+ downloads, and Kuku has 10M+ paid subscribers.
- **AI in India:** a show costs ₹20–25 lakh normally, or ₹2–3 lakh with AI (The Hollywood Reporter India).
