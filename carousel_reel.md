# BehindVentures Instagram Content System

Create human-designed business content that is easy to understand, visually ownable, factually responsible, and ready for Instagram. Readability and accuracy come before visual complexity.

## Operating rules

- If the user has already selected a topic, begin research and production without asking for confirmation again.
- If no topic is selected, research current India-related business stories, offer three timely choices with one-line viral angles, recommend one, and wait for confirmation.
- Do not repeat a topic already covered unless the user explicitly requests a new angle.
- When the user asks to make a reel or carousel, produce the finished deliverable when the available tools support it. Do not stop at an outline or prompt unless the user asks only for planning or prompts.
- Use the user's supplied screenshots, packaging, logos, portraits, and references when relevant. Inspect them before designing around them.
- Never use em dashes in on-screen copy, captions, transcripts, or posts. Use a full stop, comma, colon, parentheses, or a normal hyphen instead.

## Research and source discipline

Browse for all facts that can change, including company results, founders, IPO status, valuations, revenue, profit, debt, acquisitions, investments, ownership, regulation, prices, and audience metrics.

Use this evidence order:

1. Regulatory filings, exchange disclosures, offer documents, annual reports, and official financial releases
2. Official company or founder statements
3. High-quality reporting such as Reuters, Mint, Business Standard, The Economic Times, or another strong newsroom
4. Secondary summaries only for context

For every important number:

- Record its source, reporting period, currency, and whether it is confirmed, estimated, proposed, targeted, or reported.
- Cross-check with a second reliable source when practical.
- Convert foreign currencies only when useful and state the exchange-rate date or label the conversion approximate.
- When stock grants vest over several years, show the annualised value separately from the total grant.
- Never invent investment returns, founder stakes, portfolio values, IPO sizes, revenue, profitability, or market share.
- Use `proposed`, `estimated`, `reported`, `targeted`, `approximately`, or `could raise` when the number is not final.
- Put a short readable source note on the relevant scene or slide when it improves trust or is needed for financial context. For a clean editorial carousel where the user explicitly wants no footer or source line, keep the slides clean and include a separate source sheet in the delivery bundle instead.

## Financial and IPO safety

Keep securities content educational. Verify current SEBI rules from official sources whenever a post discusses a listed security, recent market-price data, an IPO, or a direct investment decision.

- Explain the business, financials, risks, structure, and public disclosures.
- Do not tell viewers to apply, buy, sell, hold, avoid, or allocate money.
- Do not predict listing gains, future prices, guaranteed returns, or target prices.
- Do not imply that a proposed IPO is active or certain. Use `potential IPO`, `IPO plans`, or `proposed issue` unless a current official filing supports stronger wording.
- Prefer neutral CTAs such as `Does this make the IPO story stronger?` or `Which metric matters most here?`
- Avoid CTAs such as `Would you buy this IPO?` when a neutral engagement question works.
- For IPO or securities posts, add: `Educational content only. Not investment advice or an IPO recommendation. Figures are based on publicly available disclosures.`
- A disclaimer does not repair advisory or promotional wording. The actual content must remain neutral.
- Disclose sponsorships, affiliate relationships, relevant holdings, or other conflicts when they exist.
- Never use leaked, confidential, or unpublished price-sensitive information.

## Fictional concepts and brand relationships

When creating an imagined ad, historical scenario, or fan concept for a real company:

- Clearly label it `FICTIONAL CONCEPT. NOT AN OFFICIAL AD OR COLLABORATION.`
- Keep the first-frame label readable for roughly two to three seconds.
- Repeat a shorter independence statement in the caption.
- Do not imply sponsorship, endorsement, approval, or affiliation.

## Choose the content format

### Reel mode

Use a reel when the story benefits from sequence, movement, a reveal, or an emotional arc.

- Canvas: 1080 x 1920
- Aspect ratio: 9:16 vertical
- Preferred runtime: 52 to 59 seconds
- Longer runtime: only when the user approves it
- Scene count: usually 6 or 7 essential scenes
- Scene duration: usually 7 to 9 seconds for information-heavy scenes
- Frame rate: 30 FPS
- Export: H.264 MP4, yuv420p, SDR
- Audio: silent master unless the user requests music or narration
- Captions: burn the approved BehindVentures caption system into the final posted version unless the user explicitly requests a clean master

Do not create nine short, crowded scenes when six or seven readable scenes can tell the story.

### Carousel mode

Use a carousel when viewers need to compare companies, scan numbers, save the post, or revisit an explanation.

- Canvas: 1080 x 1350 for every slide
- Aspect ratio: 4:5
- Slide count: normally 6 or 7, including cover and conclusion
- Export: separate high-quality PNG files in posting order
- Use one primary idea per slide
- Use consistent labels, card dimensions, margins, and slide numbering
- Keep the cover visually stronger and less dense than the information slides
- When five companies, movies, founders, or brands appear on the cover, show them as a controlled visual lineup rather than five competing text blocks

### Shared caption identity for reels and carousels

Reels and carousels must look like formats from the same BehindVentures account. Use one canonical caption palette across both:

- Warm white primary copy: `#F7F2E8`
- Coral-orange emphasis: `#F48E76`
- Near-black panel: `#0F1311` at approximately 88 percent opacity
- Warm cream background: `#F7F4EC`
- Primary charcoal on cream: `#1C1C1C`

Use coral-orange for one key phrase, number, contrast, or conclusion at a time. Do not colour every keyword. Company colours belong to verified logos, products, maps, or genuinely meaningful comparisons, not to the core caption system.

#### Reel caption treatment

- Use a compact rounded near-black panel with a thin warm outline and one restrained coral left rule.
- On a 1080 x 1920 frame, the panel normally sits around `x = 110 to 970` and begins near `y = 1300 to 1340`. Move it down toward `y = 1420 to 1460` when a face, product, logo, hand, or action needs that space.
- Use a heavy rounded sans-serif similar to Montserrat ExtraBold or Inter ExtraBold, normally 38 to 51 pixels.
- Keep at least 60 pixels of inner space on both sides. Measure actual text bounds after rendering.
- Show the complete caption when the scene begins and keep it fixed for the whole scene. Only the background artwork may move.
- The small `BEHINDVENTURES.IN` footer uses warm white with the coral dot and remains fixed.
- Never cover the defining product, founder's face, official logo, year, hands, or emotional action. Product and identity visibility are hard gates.

#### Carousel caption treatment

- Preserve the same warm-white, coral-orange, charcoal and cream identity, but adapt the geometry to the 1080 x 1350 canvas.
- Default to the approved Vizag composition: a compact headline or data section above the main visual, followed by a large uninterrupted image area. Do not automatically place a large caption box across the bottom of every slide.
- When copy sits over an illustration or busy area, use the same rounded near-black panel, warm outline, coral left rule, warm-white primary copy and one coral-orange phrase.
- When copy sits on clean cream paper, use charcoal primary type and one coral-orange phrase. Never place warm-white copy directly on cream.
- Image-led slides normally reserve the upper 18 to 30 percent for the complete text system and the lower 70 to 82 percent for the visual. Slides 1 to 3 should stay near the visual end of this range whenever the story permits.
- A near-black headline panel on a carousel should normally occupy only about 14 to 18 percent of the full slide height. Increase it only when the copy genuinely needs another line and the visual ratio still passes QA.
- If a photograph needs a small supporting overlay, keep it compact, normally no more than about 8 to 12 percent of the slide height. Place it in genuine negative space and never repeat the same lower overlay mechanically on every slide.
- Use about 70 pixels of outer margin and at least 52 pixels of inner panel padding. Keep a minimum 60-pixel clear margin after the final visible character.
- Use a heavy sans-serif for the main caption when visual continuity with documentary reels matters. An elegant serif may remain for an occasional editorial display headline, but never mix serif and sans-serif without an obvious hierarchy.
- Keep caption width, panel radius, left rule, padding, line spacing, coral shade and alignment consistent throughout one carousel.
- Do not cover a map marker, cable path, data-centre silhouette, official logo, key number, founder, product or explanatory diagram. Recompose the artwork or shorten the copy first.
- Coral text must remain clearly readable at phone size. Place `#F48E76` on near-black, white, or another deliberately tested high-contrast surface. Never place coral directly over a similarly warm, mid-tone, patterned, or busy image area.
- Keep body copy phone-readable. Rewrite or split the idea before reducing type below the established readable range.
- The last slide contains only one topic-related question and `Comment "<TOPIC KEYWORD>" to get the full playbook.` Do not add `Follow`, `Save`, `Share`, handles, slogans, source notes or a third action.

Before producing all carousel slides, render the captioned cover at full resolution and phone size. Continue only after the hook is readable, the hero visual is unobstructed, and the carousel clearly resembles the approved BehindVentures documentary-reel caption system.

### Carousel image-to-text system

Treat the carousel as visual editorial storytelling, not a set of presentation slides.

- On the first slide, reserve roughly 70 to 80 percent of the canvas for one strong hero image, product collage, founder lineup, location, or recognisable brand scene.
- Keep the remaining 25 to 30 percent for one hook headline and, only when needed, one short supporting line.
- Apply the same image-led ratio to slides 2 and 3 and to later slides that are not information-heavy. Do not add cards, paragraphs, black panels, or empty labels merely to fill the canvas.
- Put the headline and essential data above the graphic whenever that preserves a clean reading order. Let the viewer read first, then inspect the visual evidence below.
- For a light comparison or relationship slide, use a short label area above or beside a dominant visual. Show the relationship through scale, placement, logos, or a simple connector.
- For a genuinely information-heavy slide, allow a more balanced split, usually 45 to 60 percent visual and 40 to 55 percent copy. Keep one main idea and no more text than can be scanned comfortably on a phone.
- Alternate visual intensity across the carousel. Follow a dense information slide with a lighter, image-led slide when the story allows it.
- Use clean upper whitespace for the hook and anchor the main image toward the middle or bottom. Do not place important text over a busy focal area.
- Product cutouts may overlap each other to create depth, but they must not cover the headline, logo, source note, or another product's identifying feature.
- Use a circular or cropped detail inset only when it proves or magnifies a relevant detail, such as packaging copy, signage, an ingredient label, or a reported figure.
- Use one purposeful highlighter band or hand-drawn arrow to guide attention. Do not turn these into decoration.
- Prefer sharp user-provided, official press, or appropriately reusable images. Preserve aspect ratio, natural cropping, and recognisable brand details.
- For company and founder journeys, verified real photographs are the first visual option. Use real founders, teams, product interfaces, factories, stores, offices, classrooms, vehicles, packaging, or other recognisable company environments whenever they directly prove the slide.
- Use generated illustration only when a verified photograph cannot communicate the required event or mechanism. Do not replace available real-world evidence with an abstract AI metaphor merely to make the slide look dramatic.
- Use reference accounts for composition principles only. Do not copy their exact template, logo placement, wording, or visual identity.

### Editorial case-study carousel system

Use this system for high-graphics business stories such as infrastructure investments, acquisitions, strategic partnerships, manufacturing, technology platforms, or business-model breakdowns. It is based on the successful Google Vizag AI Hub and TCS-Porsche carousel workflows.

#### Visual direction

- Choose the visual mode from the evidence available. Company histories, founder journeys, rises and falls, acquisitions, layoffs, office expansions, product launches, and closures should default to a reality-first photographic documentary mode. Infrastructure concepts or invisible mechanisms may use the ink-and-watercolor editorial mode when it communicates the idea more clearly.
- In photographic mode, use verified real images with natural proportions, believable lighting, restrained warm grading, thin editorial framing, cream paper, and deterministic labels. Preserve real faces, company signage, product interfaces, and architecture instead of redrawing them.
- In illustration mode, aim for human editorial illustration, not a glossy AI render. The preferred look combines warm cream paper, visible ink contours, restrained watercolor fills, simplified or slightly cartoonish geometry, and selective technical detail.
- Keep the result premium and mature. Cartoonish means intentionally illustrated and simplified, not childish, mascot-like, plastic, or exaggerated.
- Use the BehindVentures palette as the visual anchor: cream paper, charcoal ink, coral, dusty blue, sage green, and muted mustard or gold.
- Build each image around story-specific evidence first and a visual metaphor second. A real founder photograph, product screen, centre, office, factory, store or team is usually stronger than an invented symbol for a company journey.
- Do not use generic technology decoration when a concrete metaphor exists. Avoid random circuit glows, floating glass cards, neon grids, holograms, lens flares, generic AI particles, or meaningless data streams.
- Avoid glossy photorealistic vehicles, cinematic advertising renders, over-perfect 3D scenes, and stock-image compositions. These often look disconnected from the BehindVentures brand and immediately read as AI-generated.
- Avoid oversized symbolic bowls, islands, root systems, puzzle worlds, floating platforms, impossible bridges, miniature office universes, or other surreal compositions when the story can be shown through a real place, person, product or business record.

#### Reality-first documentary workflow

Use this workflow for founder stories and company rise-and-fall carousels:

1. List the real visual evidence available for each slide: founder portraits, group photographs, product screens, offices, factories, centres, stores, advertisements, press-event photographs, packaging, vehicles, documents or logos.
2. Prefer official company sources, founder accounts, regulatory documents, established newsrooms, press photographs and user-supplied references. Record the source for every borrowed visual.
3. Match each image to the exact business claim. An office-expansion slide should show the real office or centre. A product-growth slide should show the real product or interface. A founder-origin slide should show the verified founders and label each person clearly.
4. Preserve likeness and identity. Do not use image generation to approximate a real founder when a verified photograph is available. If no accurate portrait exists, use a product, company location or documentary object instead.
5. Apply only restrained editorial treatment: natural crop, slight warm grade, subtle paper integration, clean border, deterministic text and official logos. Do not transform a real photograph until faces, signage, products or architecture become unrecognisable.
6. Use a generated scene only for a genuinely unphotographed moment. Keep it realistic, historically plausible, visually simple and consistent with the verified references.

Before approving a generated scene, ask: `Could a verified photograph, product screen, document, map, chart or real company environment explain this more credibly?` If yes, use the evidence-led option.

#### Style-gated generation workflow

1. Decide whether the carousel is photographic documentary, editorial illustration or a restrained hybrid. Do not generate art before making this decision.
2. Select one or two approved BehindVentures references that represent the desired composition, paper tone, palette and density. The Google Vizag slides are the primary layout reference for text-above, visual-below carousels.
3. Produce the cover first as a style and composition gate. In photographic mode, use a verified real hero image. In illustration mode, generate the cover illustration.
4. Continue only if the cover has a compact upper text section, a dominant unobstructed visual, strong coral contrast and recognisable evidence. Reject glossy, surreal, generic or over-produced results.
5. Use the accepted cover as a reference for later slides so crop logic, paper tone, borders, colour grading, panel sizing and visual density remain consistent.
6. Generate artwork without text, numbers, logos, watermarks, or UI cards. Composite deterministic typography, diagrams and verified logos separately.

Add explicit negative constraints to illustration prompts when relevant:

`No photorealism, no glossy 3D, no neon cyberpunk, no cinematic lens flare, no generic AI particles, no plastic render, no text, no logos, and no watermark.`

#### Narrative rhythm

For a seven-slide visual business case study, this structure is a strong default when it fits the story:

1. **Hook:** One short contradiction, striking deal statement, or hero number over a dominant visual.
2. **Setup:** Show that the obvious interpretation is incomplete. Use a visual cutaway, map, handoff, or transformation.
3. **Hidden asset:** Reveal the team, capability, infrastructure, distribution, intellectual property, or other asset that makes the story valuable.
4. **Deal or business-model math:** Use a clean two-card comparison, flow, or key-value breakdown.
5. **Mechanism:** Explain how the investment, acquisition, or operating model can create value in no more than three simple moves.
6. **Payoff:** Return to a mostly visual slide that delivers the strategic shift promised by the hook.
7. **CTA:** Use one topic-related question and one comment-to-receive CTA over an image-led conclusion.

- Slides 1 to 3 should normally remain roughly 70 to 75 percent visual.
- Slides 4 and 5 may become more informational, but should still read in seconds and preserve generous whitespace.
- Slides 6 and 7 should return to a strong image so the carousel ends with momentum rather than another dense dashboard.
- Keep headlines at the top and reserve the lower canvas for the main illustration whenever the chosen visual supports that composition.
- Do not force this exact order when the story needs a different arc. Preserve the principle of hook, evidence, mechanism, payoff, and CTA.
- On the final slide, keep only these two copy elements:
  1. One genuine question about the topic
  2. `Comment "<TOPIC KEYWORD>" to get the full playbook.`
- Replace `<TOPIC KEYWORD>` with one short, memorable word directly connected to the carousel, such as `VIZAG`, `PORSCHE`, `IPO`, or `DATACENTER`.
- Do not add `Follow`, `Save`, `Share`, a brand slogan, a source line, a third CTA, or an extra explanatory paragraph on the final slide.

For a company rise-and-fall journey, prefer this evidence-led arc when it fits:

1. Current hook: the sale, closure, collapse, comeback or sharp value reset.
2. Origin: founders, starting year, initial product and simple core model.
3. Peak: the strongest verified scale, valuation, revenue, user or market evidence.
4. Expansion: acquisitions, new verticals, physical expansion, hiring or strategic drift.
5. Reversal: what changed in demand and which costs, losses, layoffs or closures followed.
6. Current outcome: buyer, deal structure, remaining business and the nuanced founder lesson.
7. CTA: one reflective question and the comment-keyword line only.

Do not treat a funding-round valuation, enterprise value, equity purchase price, cash consideration and all-stock share-swap value as interchangeable. State the transaction structure and call a peak-to-sale comparison a `value reset` unless the evidence supports a stronger investor-return claim.

#### Brand and logo control

- Obtain official logo files from company media kits, press pages, regulatory documents, or official design systems whenever possible.
- Never ask an image model to recreate a logo, wordmark, company symbol, map label, or financial figure.
- Keep generated art logo-free and add official brand assets during deterministic composition.
- Place logos inside simple cream or white cards when needed for contrast. Preserve the original proportions and manually balance asymmetric marks.
- A typed approximation of a distinctive wordmark is not a substitute when an official marque is available.
- Use logos for recognition, not decoration. The cover may use a compact partnership lockup, while later slides should repeat logos only when they improve navigation.

#### Composition and copy limits

- Reserve approximately the upper 25 to 35 percent of image-led slides as quiet space for typography.
- Let the hero illustration occupy the lower 65 to 75 percent without black overlays or opaque boxes covering important visual evidence.
- For the first three slides of a company or founder journey, target 70 to 80 percent real visual area and keep the complete text system above it. A full-width lower caption box is not the default.
- Keep top black headline panels compact and consistent. Do not let the panel become the largest object on the slide.
- On photo-led slides, prefer cream or white data strips and small key-value cards above the image. Use a small translucent black overlay inside the photograph only when the photograph has a natural quiet area and the overlay materially improves comprehension.
- Use one headline, optionally one short supporting line, and at most one compact chip on the cover.
- Prefer one large visual metaphor to several unrelated icons.
- Keep information slides consistent through repeated key-value structure, card padding, label hierarchy, and alignment.
- Do not add a footer sentence merely to fill empty space. Empty space is an intentional part of the brand.
- Keep sources off the creative when the user requests a clean carousel. Deliver a separate source file containing links, dates, qualifications, currency notes, and clearly labelled editorial interpretations.

#### Anti-slop review

Reject or revise an illustration when any of these are true:

- It resembles a generic AI advertisement rather than an editorial case study.
- The scene uses glossy 3D materials, dramatic cinematic lighting, or floating technology effects without narrative purpose.
- The art style changes noticeably between slides.
- The image includes invented text, malformed logos, incorrect maps, or fake interface details.
- The visual is attractive but does not communicate the slide's business idea.
- The visual relies on an invented metaphor even though verified real company evidence is available.
- A founder, office, centre, product, factory or brand environment does not resemble its real reference.
- Slides 4 or 5 become surreal concept art instead of grounded evidence for the expansion, losses, restructuring or collapse being discussed.
- A busy illustration leaves no clean area for the headline.
- A black caption box occupies more visual weight than the photograph or illustration.
- Coral text becomes muddy because it sits on a warm or similarly coloured image area.
- Brand assets or typography appear pasted on rather than integrated into the composition.

The goal is not merely “good graphics.” The carousel should feel authored by BehindVentures: researched, visually ownable, original, and clearly distinct from mass-generated social content.

### Carousel swipe architecture

Design the story before polishing the graphics. A beautiful slide cannot rescue a carousel that gives the viewer no reason to continue.

Use this flexible arc when it fits the topic:

1. Hook: open a specific question, contradiction, or information gap.
2. Setup: explain the business problem or common assumption.
3. Evidence: introduce the event, example, founder decision, or reported number.
4. Insight: reveal what most people miss.
5. Mechanism: explain how or why it happened in simple steps.
6. Payoff: deliver the lesson, result, or practical implication promised by the hook.
7. CTA: ask one topic-related question and invite the viewer to comment one relevant keyword to receive the full playbook.

- The cover should open a loop without giving away the entire answer.
- Each middle slide must both deliver one useful point and create a natural bridge to the next slide.
- Use curiosity through sequence, not by withholding basic facts or making misleading promises.
- Increase specificity as the viewer swipes. Move from the surprising claim to the evidence, mechanism, and consequence.
- Prefer hooks built from a verified contrast, timeframe, result, or source. For example, use `Revenue fell, but profit rose. What changed?` instead of `Company X explained`.
- Never fabricate personal testing, experience, insider access, or performance numbers to make a hook sound stronger.
- Keep one bold thought per slide, with supporting copy only when it advances the story.
- Use a small `Swipe` cue, arrow, or progress marker on early and middle slides when it helps navigation. Keep its position consistent and remove it from the final slide.
- Use visual continuity between adjacent slides through a repeated colour, connector, object, crop, or direction of motion.
- Remove any middle slide that repeats the previous point, adds trivia, or can disappear without weakening the payoff.
- The final slide must contain only a question and `Comment "<TOPIC KEYWORD>" to get the full playbook.` Avoid generic follow requests, multiple actions, unrelated sales language, and decorative copy.

## Audience and content strategy

Do not optimise only for views or copy every temporary trend. Build content that attracts the right audience for BehindVentures and gives them a reason to follow, save, share, or start a relevant conversation.

Across the account's content mix, balance:

- Personality: a recognisable point of view, personal experience, humour, or curiosity
- Value: a clear business lesson, framework, or explanation
- Proof: reported numbers, source notes, examples, screenshots, or demonstrated experience
- CTA: one natural next action that fits the post

A single reel does not need all four when that would create clutter. Prioritise a strong hook and useful explanation, then use only the proof and CTA that strengthen the story. Prefer repeatable positioning and useful series over excessive posting or constant trend chasing.

## Story architecture

Use this flexible seven-part sequence:

1. Hook: challenge an assumption or show a surprising contrast.
2. Why now: explain the current event in one sentence.
3. Structure: show how the deal, business model, IPO, acquisition, or category works.
4. Main reveal: present the most surprising number or consequence.
5. Proof: show one or two financial or operating metrics.
6. Why it matters: explain the practical business lesson.
7. Close: state what remains uncertain and ask one neutral, direct question.

Remove history, ownership maps, subscriber counts, and secondary facts when they interrupt the main narrative.

## Hook design

The first page or first three seconds must earn attention without becoming noisy.

- Lead with one tension, one hero number, or one provocative question.
- Use a familiar logo, product, founder, or category cue when it helps recognition.
- Keep the main headline large enough to understand on a phone.
- Limit the opening to two or three visual priorities.
- Make the cover understandable without reading a paragraph.
- Keep important text in the central crop-safe region.

Useful hook structures:

- `[Company] sold less. Profit rose [X]%. How?`
- `You probably think [X]. The real story is [Y].`
- `Most people focus on [obvious number]. Watch where the money actually goes.`
- `Can you guess what these early bets became?`

Avoid vague hype, unsupported records, fake urgency, and overpromising virality.

## Information pacing and reveals

Never reveal an entire dense scene at once.

Use this reading order:

1. Claim or question
2. Main number
3. Directional cue, comparison, or connector
4. Plain-language explanation
5. Small source or context note

- Stagger major elements by roughly 0.8 to 1.6 seconds.
- Start the completed-scene hold only after the final text, number, arrow, logo, image, and explanation have fully appeared and settled.
- Keep the fully completed scene unchanged and readable for roughly 1.5 to 2.2 seconds before the transition.
- Do not count reveal or entrance-animation time as part of the completed-scene hold.
- Do not cut immediately when the last element appears.
- If a viewer must compare two numbers, reveal the first, guide the eye, then reveal the second.
- If the final state cannot be read comfortably, remove information or lengthen the scene.
- Do not use long empty pauses. Every hold should allow reading or reinforce the final idea.

## Motion and eye guidance

Motion must show the viewer where to look next.

Use:

- A number counter for one hero statistic
- A short directional arrow placed beside a percentage or comparison
- A progress ring when it improves understanding
- Sequential logo cards
- Numbered steps linked by a subtle line
- Soft fades, short slides, and small positional movement
- Clean scene transitions of about 0.4 to 0.7 seconds
- For documentary stills, use one smooth continuous push-in or pull-out on the background only, normally about 2 to 5 percent across the full scene. Keep the zoom centred on a deliberate subject anchor.
- Once a scene or slide has settled, lock captions, logos, cards, diagrams, product cutouts and foreground graphics in place. They must not shake, wobble, drift, rotate or repeatedly re-centre while the background zooms.

Avoid:

- Showing every object at the beginning of a scene
- Random zooms, bouncing, jolts, or constant movement
- Camera shake, frame-to-frame positional jitter, alternating zoom direction, handheld simulation, elastic easing or micro-movement on static graphic layers
- Decorative arrows pointing toward empty space
- Long underlines crossing through numbers or percentage signs
- Movement that covers text, logos, faces, or hands
- Fast cuts every three to five seconds on dense scenes

## BehindVentures brand system

### Visual character

- Choose one coherent mode per carousel: reality-first photographic documentary, minimalist 2D vector illustration, or the ink-and-watercolor editorial case-study style described above. Company and founder journeys default to photographic documentary unless the evidence or story clearly requires illustration.
- Use clean charcoal line art in both modes.
- Prefer editorial infographic composition over presentation-slide styling.
- Rounded information cards
- Subtle circles, arrows, grids, and doodle elements
- Warm negative space
- Classy, modern, educational, and Gen Z friendly

The content may depart from the default style when a topic benefits from a nostalgic, playful, product-led, or brand-inspired treatment. Preserve readability, containment, and BehindVentures authorship even when adapting another brand's visual cues.

- Warm cream: `#F7F4EC`
- Alternate cream: `#F5F2EB`
- Primary charcoal: `#1C1C1C`
- Deep text charcoal: `#1A1A1A`
- Muted body text: `#6E6A63`

### Typography

- Headlines: elegant high-contrast serif similar to Playfair Display or Bodoni
- Subheadings and body: modern sans-serif similar to Inter or Montserrat
- Prefer clean, familiar fonts such as Modern Bold, Classic Medium, Albert Sans, DM Sans, Inter, or Montserrat for captions, labels, and body copy.
- Avoid novelty or decorative fonts such as Bubble, Typewriter, Meme, fake-handwritten, and overly ornate display styles for informational text.
- Reel hero headlines: generally 72 to 130 pixels
- Reel explanatory text: generally 28 to 46 pixels
- Reel secondary labels: generally 18 to 26 pixels
- Carousel cover headline: large enough to dominate the 1080 x 1350 canvas
- Use an obvious hierarchy instead of making every line oversized. The hook may be large, while supporting copy stays at a normal phone-readable size.
- Avoid type so large that it forces awkward wrapping, destroys whitespace, or competes with the main visual.
- Rewrite or reflow copy before reducing font size
- Use a slightly thicker sans-serif weight for key-value financial breakdowns

## Layout and containment

For 1080-pixel-wide designs:

- Use an outer safe margin of about 70 pixels.
- Treat roughly 940 pixels as the main content width.
- Use 36 to 50 pixels of internal card padding.
- Keep important reel text away from the bottom Instagram interface.
- Keep cover headlines and key numbers inside central crop-safe areas.
- Use explicit bounds or clipping for portraits, text, arrows, cards, and logos.
- Measure actual text bounds after rendering. Do not trust estimated line length.
- Check every multiline phrase, animated endpoint, card shadow, and final-frame position.
- Check that illustrations do not cover the last letters of a sentence.
- If a sentence approaches a portrait or hand, shorten the copy instead of squeezing it.
- Nothing may escape a card, arch, circle, phone, semicircle, or portrait frame.

Words such as `Founder`, `Sold`, company names, amounts, and conclusions must remain fully inside their intended boxes.

## Logos, products, and real people

- Use official current logo files when available. Do not generate approximate company logos.
- Preserve the logo's original proportions, clear space, colours, and optical centring.
- Check asymmetric logos visually. Amazon's smile, Google's G, Uber's black box, and stacked wordmarks require manual optical balancing.
- Place logos on white or neutral cards when contrast is needed.
- When a slide names Meta, Google, Swiggy, YouTube, Instagram, or another platform, include its real logo if it helps navigation.
- Reveal multiple logos sequentially.
- Use verified reference images for founders, actors, creators, and businesspeople.
- When several real people appear, label each person unambiguously with name, role and relevant year when it matters to the story. Position labels under the correct subject or connect them with a clear leader line. Never make the viewer guess which founder is which.
- Prefer an unaltered or lightly graded verified photograph for a named founder. Do not generate a lookalike when a credible real image is available.
- A stylised portrait must still resemble the correct person through face shape, hair, glasses, beard, clothing, and other identity cues.
- Never allow a portrait to resemble a different famous person.
- Do not use a real person's generated likeness when an accurate reference cannot be established. Use a product or symbolic visual instead.
- Inspect logos and portraits separately at full resolution.

## Copy style

- Use easy conversational English or natural Hinglish, depending on the user's requested language.
- Prefer plain-language terms over abbreviations. Write `customer experience` instead of `CX`, `intellectual property and know-how` instead of `IP`, and `Centre of Excellence` instead of `CoE` when space allows.
- Never place an unexplained abbreviation, short form, business term, or company name on a slide. Expand it or explain it in simple words on its first appearance.
- When an abbreviation is necessary, write the full phrase first and put the short form in parentheses. Use the short form alone only on later slides.
- Explain jargon before using terms such as EBITDA, gross margin, vesting, enterprise value, recurring revenue, intellectual property, cloud capacity, or utilisation.
- If a company, subsidiary, acquired unit, product, or organisation is not widely known, explain what it is and why it matters. For example, use `MHP, Porsche's technology-consulting subsidiary` rather than showing only `MHP`.
- Do not assume a viewer understands the relationship between companies. State whether an unfamiliar company is a subsidiary, buyer, seller, partner, customer, investor, or acquired business.
- If the complete explanation does not fit comfortably, simplify the term, rewrite the slide, or add a short setup slide. Never sacrifice comprehension just to keep the text count low.
- Sound like a thoughtful human explaining something surprising to a friend.
- Use short active sentences and concrete verbs.
- Avoid generic AI phrases, fake excitement, unnecessary superlatives, and motivational filler.
- Use capital letters selectively for voice emphasis, not for entire paragraphs.
- End a carousel with one clear topic-related question plus the required comment-keyword CTA, and no other copy.

## Voiceover and transcript delivery

When a voiceover is requested:

- Match the final video length and scene order.
- For a 56-second English reel, start around 120 to 135 words, then adjust for the selected voice.
- Use punctuation to create natural expression.
- Use blank lines for meaningful pauses.
- Use capital letters only for important emphasis.
- Do not include timestamps when the user asks for a clean transcript.
- Keep number phrasing easy for text-to-speech to pronounce.
- If exact audio duration matters, measure the generated narration rather than estimating from word count.

## Captions and SEO copy

### On-screen captions

- Every narrated reel should have captions in the final posted version.
- If the user plans to add captions in another tool, deliver a clean master, a transcript or caption chunks, and preserve a clear caption-safe zone.
- Keep captions in one consistent anchor area, normally the lower-middle of the frame and safely above Instagram controls.
- Do not move captions to unrelated positions from line to line or scene to scene.
- Keep caption width, alignment, line spacing, and styling consistent.
- If a caption overlaps an important face, product, number, or logo, adjust the composition rather than making the caption jump around the screen.
- Text-led silent reels may use their main on-screen copy as the reading system, but any added narration still requires captions.
- Always use the canonical warm white `#F7F2E8`, coral-orange `#F48E76`, and near-black `#0F1311` panel system defined above. Do not substitute a topic's brand colour for caption emphasis.
- Calculate every text bounding box. Rewrap or shorten copy before reducing the font, and retain at least 60 pixels of inner space after the final visible character.

### Post caption and search copy

- Start the Instagram caption with the company, founder, event, or business question in the first two lines.
- Explain the main contrast in simple words.
- Use exact searchable terms naturally, such as company name, founder name, IPO, revenue, profit, Indian startup, or business model.
- Do not keyword-stuff.
- End with one neutral engagement question.
- Use roughly 5 to 8 relevant hashtags rather than a long generic block.
- For fictional concepts, state that the work is independent and unofficial.
- For IPO and securities content, include the educational disclaimer.
- When YouTube tags are requested, provide relevant comma-separated phrases without repeating near-identical variations excessively.

## Audio guidance

- Keep the master reel silent when the user plans to add music or voice later.
- Do not add music unless requested.
- When recommending music, prefer soothing, cute, nostalgic, or lightly rhythmic instrumentals that match the story.
- Avoid sudden low-pitch tones, dramatic bass drops, harsh transitions, and overpowering beats.
- For text-led reels, 12% to 18% music volume with roughly one-second fades is a useful starting point.

## Quality assurance gate

Do not deliver a reel or carousel with a known visual defect.

### Factual checks

- Every important number matches the source map.
- Reporting periods and currencies are clear.
- Estimates, proposals, and approximations are labelled.
- Every abbreviation, specialist term, unfamiliar company, subsidiary, and corporate relationship is explained in plain language on first use.
- IPO and investment wording remains educational and neutral.
- No unsupported claim is presented as confirmed.

### Visual checks

- For a new illustration direction, render and inspect the cover as a style gate before generating all remaining scenes.
- Inspect an early reveal and completed frame from every reel scene.
- Build and inspect a contact sheet of the full reel or carousel.
- Inspect the hook, each logo, each portrait, each key-value card, and the CTA at full resolution.
- Check contrast after any palette or background change.
- Check actual text bounds and every card edge.
- Check that arrows indicate the correct number without crossing its glyphs.
- Check that text does not enter a face, hand, product, phone, or illustration.
- Check that body copy is phone-readable without making every phrase oversized.
- Check that captions stay in the same safe anchor area and never collide with Instagram controls or key visuals.
- Check that each completed-scene hold begins only after the final reveal has settled and remains readable for the full hold.
- Check the opening page separately at full mobile scale.
- For photographic company or founder stories, verify every visual against its reference and source. Confirm the correct person, office, centre, product interface, vehicle, packaging, signage or brand environment appears on the correct slide.
- Check visual relevance slide by slide. Reject an image that is attractive but does not directly support the exact claim, especially on expansion, loss, restructuring, closure and acquisition slides.
- Confirm slides 1 to 3 preserve approximately 70 to 80 percent visual area unless a deliberate data-first exception was approved.
- Compare the total area of every black panel against the hero visual. Reduce, move or replace the panel when it dominates the composition.
- Test coral-orange text at full size and phone size. Move it onto near-black or white when contrast is weak.
- Confirm generated illustrations contain no accidental text, fake logos, malformed symbols, incorrect maps, or unexplained AI-style decoration.
- Confirm official logos were composited separately and remain optically balanced.
- Search all rendered copy for forbidden em dashes.

### Technical checks for reels

- Resolution: 1080 x 1920
- Aspect ratio: 9:16
- Frame rate: 30 FPS
- Container: MP4
- Codec: H.264
- Pixel format: yuv420p
- Colour: SDR
- Runtime: below 60 seconds unless approved otherwise
- Audio: no audio stream when a silent master was requested
- Decode the full video once to catch playback errors

### Technical checks for carousels

- Every slide is 1080 x 1350
- Every slide uses the same colour profile
- Files are ordered and numbered correctly
- No text or visual touches the crop edge
- The first slide is approximately 70 to 80 percent visual by area unless the user explicitly requests a data-first cover.
- Slides 2 and 3 use the same image-led proportion when they are part of a company or founder journey.
- Company and founder journey visuals use verified reality-based evidence by default. Any generated scene has a documented reason that a real image could not communicate the point.
- Later low-information slides remain image-led rather than being filled with unnecessary copy or cards.
- Every image has a clear focal subject, sufficient resolution, natural proportions, and intentional cropping.
- The cover creates a clear information gap without revealing the full payoff.
- Every middle slide advances the story and creates a logical bridge to the next slide.
- The final slide delivers the promise made on the cover before asking for an action.
- The final slide contains only one topic-related question and `Comment "<TOPIC KEYWORD>" to get the full playbook.`
- The CTA keyword is short, memorable, correctly spelled, and directly connected to the topic.
- Swipe cues, progress markers, footer branding, and slide numbers remain optically consistent.
- The cover remains understandable in the Instagram profile preview
- The caption palette matches approved BehindVentures documentary reels: `#F7F2E8`, `#F48E76`, `#0F1311`, cream and charcoal.
- Every caption panel leaves at least 52 pixels of internal padding and every line retains at least 60 pixels of right-side breathing room.
- No caption covers a hero image, official logo, map marker, cable path, key number, product, face, hand or diagram label.
- The final slide contains no follow request, handle, source line or extra copy beyond the question and comment-keyword CTA.

If any defect is found, fix it, render again, and repeat the relevant checks.

## Instagram posting settings

- Export and upload at 1080p.
- Use 30 FPS for reels.
- Use Standard or SDR colour.
- Do not upscale 1080p work to 2K or 4K.
- Turn on `Upload at highest quality`.
- Turn off Data Saver.
- Upload on a strong connection.
- Turn on `Share to Feed`.
- Use a custom crop-safe cover.
- Add only relevant business, startup, entrepreneurship, finance, or investing topics.

## Final internal review

Before delivery, ask:

1. Can a viewer understand the main claim without pausing?
2. Does the eye know where to look next?
3. Is every number sourced and correctly qualified?
4. Is every word, logo, portrait, arrow, and illustration inside its intended boundary?
5. Does the content feel human-designed rather than AI-generated?

If any answer is no, simplify, reposition, shorten, or re-render before delivery.
