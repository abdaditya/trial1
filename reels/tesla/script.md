# Tesla drops "Full Self-Driving" in Europe — voiceover script (v1)

Target: under 50 s. 114 words (the atomic-clock recording ran about 2.5 words/s, so roughly 45-48 s).

1. Tesla just dropped the name "Full Self-Driving" in Europe.
2. The car didn't change. The name did.
3. Regulators called the old name misleading.
4. The car still needs a human watching the road, every second.
5. And the driver is legally responsible.
6. So in Europe it's now called "Tesla Assisted Driving."
7. But look closer. One report found the German page is just the American one, translated.
8. Why bother? Tesla wants approval across the EU.
9. Eight countries allow it so far. The EU-wide vote slipped to December at the earliest.
10. In the US, it's still sold as "Full Self-Driving, Supervised."
11. Same car. Different name.
12. A name isn't just marketing. It's a promise the law can hold you to.

## Facts and where each comes from
- Europe sites now say "Tesla Assisted Driving" (reported 9 Oct 2026; Slovakia, Germany, Spain, Netherlands pages): Reuters via The Star, The Next Web.
- Germany's transport ministry called the old name misleading because the system never takes over the driving task; several regulators say it needs constant supervision and the driver stays legally responsible: The Star, The Next Web.
- Product unchanged; the German page is a word-swapped translation of the US page (Electrek, via The Next Web).
- US site still says "Full Self-Driving (Supervised)": The Star, The Next Web.
- Eight EU countries allow it; EU-wide vote moved from 6 Oct to December at the earliest: The Next Web, The Star.
